# Research sources, scope and reuse

**Accessed: 2026-09-11.** All links below were consulted online on this date, including the
linked SCA standard and ICO/Uganda PDFs. A publication's own date is distinct from the
access date. Local working copies used for research are ignored and are not redistributed.

The project provides original bilingual educational explanations. Source links indicate
the scope described below, not endorsement of every adjacent recipe, comparison exercise
or sensory association. Findings and original exercises are deliberately distinguished.

## Sensory language, assessment and science

| Source | What it supports here | Copyright / limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [World Coffee Research — Sensory Lexicon](https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/resources/sensory-lexicon) | Shared sensory vocabulary, separating attributes, calibration using reference materials, the published lexicon's 110-attribute scope | The site describes free downloading/printing of **personal-use copies**. That is not permission to reproduce its definitions, standard reference recipes or wheel artwork in a commercial website. Our 96 descriptors, 8/24 hierarchy, definitions, colors and household exercises are independently written. |
| [WCR Coffee Varieties Catalog](https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/), including [Typica](https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/varieties/typica) | Species and cultivar relationships, agronomic context, differences between cultivar names and guaranteed cup outcomes | Factual paraphrases and links only; no catalog photos, tables or copied descriptions. Local landrace names are not reduced to a single cultivar without evidence. |
| [SCA Coffee Value Assessment](https://sca.coffee/value-assessment) | Distinguishing descriptive assessment from affective assessment; physical and extrinsic/informational dimensions of value | SCA material is copyrighted. We do not reproduce official forms or claim CVA certification. Our 1–5 journal intensity and separate liking scale are personal exercises, not official CVA scoring. |
| [SCA Coffee Standards](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards) | Current preparation/tasting context and the identity of 102-2024, rather than presenting an old protocol as the only current standard | Links and original factual summaries only. | [SCA 102-2024 — Sample Preparation and Tasting Mechanics](https://sca.coffee/s/AW_SCA-102_Sample-Preparation_281024_Secured.pdf), especially sample preparation and Table 2 | 8.25 g per 150 mL full vessel capacity, water 93±3°C, 3–5 minute crust; cupping-water hardness/alkalinity/pH | The publication requires consent for reproduction. We provide short original paraphrases of numerical facts and no pages, diagrams, standard forms or copied tables. Our teaching workflow fixes 4 minutes within the interval for consistency. |
| [Coffee Quality Institute — Processing education](https://www.coffeeinstitute.org/education/processing) | Post-harvest processing, intentional process control, drying/fermentation context and the need to evaluate outcomes | General framework, not a copied CQI course or claimed qualification. The practice notes and troubleshooting sequences are editorial. |
| [Batali, Ristenpart & Guinard (2020), Scientific Reports 10, 16450](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-73341-4), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-73341-4) | Strength/extraction distinction and a controlled drip-brewing study comparing 87, 90 and 93°C at fixed strength/extraction | **CC BY 4.0.** Full author attribution is retained; summaries/translations are adaptations, not verbatim text. No figures copied. The narrow finding does not prove that temperature never matters across methods or outside those conditions. General grind/flow troubleshooting in the app is an editorial exercise, not an experiment from this paper. |

In SCA 102-2024 Table 2, **calcium hardness is 50–175 ppm as CaCO₃** and **alkalinity is near
40–70 ppm as CaCO₃**, with pH 6–8 and no chlorine. These are cupping-water references.
Calcium hardness, total hardness, alkalinity, pH and a conductivity-meter TDS reading are
not interchangeable; equipment manufacturers can impose different water requirements.

Coffee aroma, basic taste, sourness, sweetness, bitterness, body, aftertaste and balance
are described separately. Flavor association does not mean added flavoring. A strong
descriptor, acidity value, species, origin or fermentation label is not inherently a high
or low quality score. The historic extraction-yield range of 18–22% is presented as a
reference window, never a universal optimum.

Household reference exercises help build vocabulary. They are not calibrated WCR intensity
standards. Unpleasant descriptors such as phenolic, moldy or chemical are explained as
associations; exercises do not instruct users to taste chemicals, spoilage or allergens.

Every origin's elevation interval is an **illustrative growing band**. Named varieties and
processes are examples that may be encountered, not prevalence estimates. Sensory links
and 1–5 acidity/body matching hints are our editorial choices. They do not measure a country,
support a quality ranking or replace a producer's traceability information and actual tasting.
Cultivar genetics, microclimate, altitude, ripeness, drying, storage, roast and brewing all interact.

## Brewing methods and numerical starting points

| Primary guide | What we use | Reuse / recipe distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [HARIO — Recipes & Guides](https://www.hario-usa.com/pages/recipes-and-guides) | Brewer/filter construction, basic handling, pour-over and siphon context | Original diagrams and steps; our selected doses, temperatures and times are practice ranges. |
| [CHEMEX — How to Brew](https://chemexcoffeemaker.com/pages/how-to-brew-with-chemex) | Three-layer filter orientation toward the spout, venting, rinse and brewing sequence | Manufacturer's wording/photos are not copied. Our 30 g / 480 g recipe is an editorial starting point. |
| [AeroPress — How to Use](https://aeropress.com/pages/how-to-use) | Upright brewing, gentle pressing and the maker's approximately 85°C recipe | Our 15 g / 210 g full-cup exercise is distinguished from that recipe. No inverted-method requirement or official illustration. |
| [Bialetti — Moka Express](https://www.bialetti.com/it_en/moka-express.html) | Room-temperature fill water, safety-valve level, untamped basket, low heat and stopping in time | Our 20–25°C range refers only to **starting boiler water**. Capacity and the actual model's manual override the calculator. No copyrighted manufacturer image used. |

The following values are editable **starting points**, not uniquely correct standards:

| Method | Default dry coffee / mass ratio | Temperature | Time interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V60 | 15 g, 1:16 input water | 90–96°C | 2.5–3.5 min total |
| Chemex | 30 g, 1:16 input water | 90–96°C | 4–5.5 min total |
| AeroPress | 15 g, 1:14 input water | 80–95°C | 1.5–2.5 min, includes press |
| French press | 20 g, 1:15 input water | 90–96°C | 4–8 min steep/settle |
| Espresso | 18 g, 1:2 **beverage yield** | 90–94°C setpoint | 25–35 s from pump-on; machine/preinfusion dependent |
| Moka pot | Approx. 18 g, 1:10 input water | 20–25°C **starting boiler water** | Approx. 3–5 min; capacity/heat dependent |
| Siphon | 20 g, 1:15 input water | 90–95°C upper chamber | 1–2 min after grounds are added, excludes heating |
| Cold brew | 50 g, 1:8 input water | 2–4°C refrigerated | 12–18 h; concentrate, dilute to taste |
| Turkish | 7 g, 1:10 input water | Approx. 90–96°C endpoint | 2–4 min gentle heating; avoid vigorous boil |
| Kalita flat-bottom | 20 g, 1:16 input water | 90–96°C | 3–4 min total |

Non-manufacturer-specific French-press, espresso, cold-brew, Turkish and Kalita recipes are
editorial teaching recipes. References support extraction concepts and evaluation, not a
claim that SCA, CQI or a paper prescribed these precise recipes. Grind descriptions are
relative particle-size metaphors; they cannot be mapped reliably to universal grinder clicks.
Filter/input-water ratios do not predict exact beverage yield because grounds retain water.
Cold-brew instructions use refrigeration and prompt consumption, not an unverified commercial
shelf-life claim. “Low acid” is not treated as acid-free or a health benefit.

## Updating the research

Revisit standards when SCA versions change and cultivar information when WCR revises its
catalog. Keep historical reports dated. Add a new source only after reading the actual
document; a successful HTTP status on a generic landing page is not evidence of a claim.
When a source becomes unavailable, preserve its title, date and scope and seek a direct
replacement. New statements must be translated, assigned a source or clearly marked as
editorial, and checked with the graph/locale tests.
